# Preface

The Anteprimicosm Framework began as an attempt to investigate one of philosophy's oldest questions:

**What are the conceptual conditions that make describable reality possible?**

This work does not propose a new scientific theory, nor does it seek to replace established cosmology or physics. Instead, it explores a philosophical domain that lies prior to scientific description—the conceptual foundations that allow reality to become meaningful and describable.

The framework introduces several original concepts, including Anteprimicosm, Harmogenesis, the Bereshit Event, and the Universe in Reverse. Together, these ideas provide a structured vocabulary for examining the relationship between ontology, emergence, complexity, and the limits of human understanding.

Readers are encouraged to approach this work as an open philosophical investigation rather than a finished doctrine. The framework is intended to stimulate discussion, invite criticism, and evolve through continued reflection and scholarly dialogue.

This repository serves as the living manuscript of the Anteprimicosm Framework. As research progresses, chapters may be revised, expanded, or reorganized while preserving the central objective of exploring the conceptual foundations of describable reality.

If this work encourages even one reader to reconsider the deepest assumptions about existence, description, and reality, then it has fulfilled its purpose.
